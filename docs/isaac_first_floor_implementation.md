#### Implementation order with early human play checks

The revised order below prioritizes catching that failure. It replaces the previous damage-first rollout order; the importance of Isaac damage/HP/range balance is unchanged.

This is the proposed execution/review process, not authorization to implement unselected IDs. Before starting a stage, explicitly select its IDs and any required subscopes. Within a stage, implement one small behavior change at a time. **Inertia clarification from the user:** do not equate native velocity damping with noticeable sliding. The 590-state bounded native fixture does retain velocity after input release; it does not establish the live game's effective stopping distance/time or prove that the current responsive stopping must change. Keep the present stopping behavior in the baseline and first speed comparison. Any S05 change requires a matched live stop test, a demonstrated difference and a separate human-reviewed candidate. No inertia/glide behavior has been added.

**Movement checkpoint checklist:** straight walking, diagonals, mouse click-to-walk, stopping, reversal, dodge, firing while moving and camera rotation. A short fixed-camera baseline/candidate comparison should expose excessive speed immediately. Keep the current eight-direction firing and mouse/keyboard mapping intact. Record room-crossing time and distance (or fraction of room width) per real second, rather than relying on how fast an animation appears.

Explicitly check the source-to-world conversion and the distinction between 30 Hz and 60 Hz updates. A per-call native trace does not by itself establish real-time movement speed; do not multiply it by a guessed scale/update frequency. Automated checks at different render rates must show consistent simulation movement. An unexplained large change, such as twice the crossing speed, blocks integration rather than being explained away as fidelity. Intended differences still require a measured reference and human acceptance.

**Before every human review, deliver something concrete:**

- An interactive baseline and candidate, each with a single launch command or a clearly labelled A/B switch, using the same seed/room, camera, zoom and spawn. These review demos are a deliverable of future selected work; this report does not claim they have already been built.
- A short list of exactly what changed and the relevant measurements: crossing time/speed for movement; travel/reach for projectiles; intervals for firing; hits/kill times for damage/HP. Present known uncertainty alongside the measurements.
- Completed relevant automated checks, a baseline code/config identifier, and a straightforward reversible way to return to the baseline. Keep local comparison binaries out of Git.
- A persisted human result: **pending**, **accepted**, or **needs revision**, tied to that exact candidate. Passing tests, a screenshot, elapsed time or silence does not count as human acceptance. If the candidate needs revision, fix that stage and repeat its check before proceeding.

**Reopen earlier checks when their inputs change.** A later coordinate-scale, collider, frame-scheduling or movement change invalidates earlier movement approval; repeat the movement A/B before continuing. A later item that changes movement or shot speed gets its own corresponding speed/range check. This catches regressions while the change is small, rather than relying on one final expedition playtest.
